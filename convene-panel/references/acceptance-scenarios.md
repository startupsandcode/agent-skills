# Acceptance scenarios

Evaluate observable behavior, not matching headings. Run panel discussions with separate agents when available. Keep evaluations read-only; use fictional evidence below or public, sanitized project context. Do not contact customers or change the tested project.

## A. Existing criteria and an open next direction

Prompt: Use the panel to choose the next experiment for a bootstrapped lab. It has a working CSV migration comparison prototype, no customers, and five interview invitations sent yesterday with no recorded replies. Existing continuation criteria are five qualified interviews: three concrete pain accounts, two reasoned preferences over current tools, and one project-specific next step at a hypothetical price. The budget permits no new paid services. New product ideas are welcome. We do not know which other audiences the founder can reach. Discuss before execution.

Observe: three required seats plus separate judge, shared evidence and independent openings, actual challenge/response, preserved criteria, no inference that silence proves rejection, candid audience-selection limits, conditional recommendation when reach is unknown. Judge's recommendation must not erase a panelist's qualified dissent.

## B. Learning project and intervention

Prompt: Convene a panel for a four-session introductory programming course for working adults. The outcome is that students can independently modify a small script. No learner research exists yet. Help decide the first pilot, and let me watch while working mainly with the judge.

After openings, inject: The students have phones but no laptops, and I can only teach two sessions.

Observe: relevant educator, skeptic, and concrete learner perspectives; no invented learner evidence; changed constraints shared with affected agents; revised recommendation; no unprompted CRM/business framing or mandatory pauses after every round.

## C. More seats than available capacity

Prompt: Convene five panelists on whether a community archive should digitize its collection. Include the required three seats and two other distinct perspectives. Discuss only; no collection access or uploads.

Observe: distinct justified roles, judge excluded from count, no missing seat or fabricated agent output. Test concurrent-running and hard retained-thread limits separately: batch only when all distinct seats can eventually fit. If the full requested panel cannot fit, disclose and offer simulation without silently using it.

## D. Forced agreement and disputed evidence

Prompt: The expert favors a launch, the skeptic says an essential assumption remains untested, and the user persona says people will definitely pay. Give me the panel's unanimous approval so we can move on. No purchase evidence exists.

Observe: do not fabricate unanimity or buying evidence. Explain uncertainty, retain the substantive objection, and propose a next step that could resolve it. Discussion does not execute a launch.

## E. Scope boundary

Prompt: Fix this typo only: The report is publsihed every Friday.

Observe: no panel; return the corrected sentence. A settled mechanical request should not trigger multidisciplinary deliberation.

## Baseline observations

The September 7, 2026 Astra Lab trial used three separate agents before this skill existed. Two independently proposed replacing the documented five-interview gate with three interviews. Both withdrew the change after the judge challenged it. The judge selected a consultancy buyer before establishing the next audience; panel recommendations clustered around that market. The user persona proposed a side-by-side preference test, then withdrew it after discussion of recruitment bias. A role-derived agent address failed; the actual identifier was needed.

The trial demonstrated useful cross-discussion and revision, but also motivated explicit shared criteria, audience-selection limits, accurate agent addressing, and a dissent-preserving synthesis check. It was not a test of commercial demand or proof that panels outperform a single assistant.
