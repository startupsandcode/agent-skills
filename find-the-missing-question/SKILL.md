# Find the Missing Question

Find the uncertainty whose answer would most change the next action. Make it answerable cheaply, then return to the user's work.

## Decide whether to intervene

Start from the user's intended outcome, stated constraints, and evidence already available. Separate the desired outcome from the proposed method and its proxy metrics. A completed artifact, a passing check, or a requested feature may be evidence of progress without establishing the intended benefit.

Use a brief assumption check when the user requests it or the supplied evidence exposes a consequential gap. For a clear mechanical task, perform the task. For a settled decision with relevant supporting evidence, help execute it; do not reopen it merely because alternatives exist.

Missing evidence means uncertainty, not proof the plan is wrong. Preserve explicit preferences and constraints. Do not substitute a different goal, make ordinary work wait for a strategy review, or manufacture a clever objection.

## Choose one question

Consider what must be true for the proposed action to achieve the outcome. Look for an unsupported causal link, a metric that excludes important cases, a solution chosen before the problem is understood, or an apparent constraint that has not been established.

Select the question with the greatest consequence for the current decision and a feasible way to resolve it. Before surfacing it, identify two plausible answers and the different actions they would justify. If both answers lead to the same action, prefer another question or proceed with the work.

Ground the question in a specific supplied fact or absence. Phrase it neutrally enough that the original plan could be supported. Do not turn guesses about customers, motives, or hidden causes into facts.

## Turn it into a decision

For an explicit review, give a compact response containing:

- **Missing question:** one answerable question.
- **Why it matters:** the evidence gap and the decision it affects.
- **Smallest test:** an existing artifact to inspect or a bounded comparison; specify what to observe and a time or sample limit.
- **Decision rule:** what would support the current plan, what would favor changing it, and what to do if the result is inconclusive.

Use existing evidence first. Compare alternatives under similar conditions when claiming improvement; a successful run alone does not establish that an intervention helped. Label proposed thresholds as choices, not proven standards. Keep small samples exploratory and scope conclusions to what was observed.

When this is an incidental observation during another task, compress it to a short note and continue independent authorized work. Ask the user only when their answer is necessary and cannot be obtained from available evidence. Execute a test when it is within the authorized task and available resources; otherwise offer the concrete test without treating its proposal as permission for external actions.

Stop after the highest-value check. If the evidence already resolves the question, state the resulting action. If no consequential gap is supported, say so briefly when explicitly asked; otherwise just complete the task.

For maintenance checks, use [acceptance scenarios](references/acceptance-scenarios.md).
