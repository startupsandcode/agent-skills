# Prepare for Interview

Build a truthful, role-specific preparation packet that improves the candidate's decisions in the conversation. Do not invent company facts, credentials, accomplishments, or interviewer context.

## Establish the interview

Identify the role, company, interview type, date and timezone, interviewer names or backgrounds if supplied, job description, and the candidate's relevant experience. Ask only for details that materially affect preparation; state unknowns plainly.

## Produce the packet

Create a concise packet in this order:

1. **Thesis:** why this role and why this candidate, grounded in supplied evidence.
2. **Role map:** the likely problems, relevant experience, and gaps to address honestly.
3. **Stories:** 3-5 STAR-style stories with the candidate's actual context, action, result, and lesson. Mark any missing evidence rather than fabricating a metric.
4. **Questions:** thoughtful questions that clarify mandate, success measures, team, constraints, and decision process. Avoid questions answerable from supplied material.
5. **Practice:** likely questions, concise answer outlines, and one or two risks or sensitive topics to rehearse.
6. **Logistics:** time, format, preparation needed, and a short pre-interview checklist when known.

## Evidence rules

Separate facts, inferences, and unknowns. Research externally only when authorized and cite sources in the packet. Do not claim knowledge of an interviewer, company priority, interview agenda, or compensation range without evidence.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Generic advice detached from the role | Tie every recommendation to the role or supplied experience |
| Invented metrics or polished stories | Preserve uncertainty and ask for the missing result later |
| Too many questions | Select the few that change the candidate's decision or preparation |
| Treating practice as a script | Use outlines and proof points in the candidate's own voice |
