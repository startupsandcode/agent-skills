# Prepare for Interview

Build a truthful, role-specific preparation packet that improves the candidate's decisions in the conversation. Do not invent company facts, credentials, accomplishments, or interviewer context.

## Establish the interview

Use supplied material and relevant workspace context first. Identify the role, company, interview type, date and timezone, interviewer details if supplied, job description, and relevant candidate experience. Treat old interview notes as historical until the current target is established.

Ask only for missing details that materially change preparation, usually the target role and job description plus relevant experience. Do useful work with partial input and label unknowns. When the user requests no clarification or has only minutes left, produce a compact brief from available evidence.

Match the requested mode: a preparation packet by default, a short brief under time pressure, or interactive mock practice when requested. Preparing an interviewer to evaluate candidates is a different task.

## Produce the packet

Use [the packet template](references/packet-template.md) for a full packet, adapting its length to the available time and evidence. Keep this order:

1. **Thesis:** why this role and why this candidate, grounded in supplied evidence.
2. **Role map:** the likely problems, relevant experience, and gaps to address honestly.
3. **Stories:** up to 3-5 distinct STAR-style stories when supported, with actual context, responsibility, personal action, result, and lesson. Use fewer when evidence is thin. Mark missing results and lessons; distinguish proposed talking points from verified experience.
4. **Questions:** thoughtful questions that clarify mandate, success measures, team, constraints, and decision process. Avoid questions answerable from supplied material.
5. **Practice:** likely questions, concise answer outlines, and one or two risks or sensitive topics to rehearse.
6. **Logistics:** time, format, preparation needed, and a short pre-interview checklist when known.

For a last-minute brief, prioritize a short introduction, the strongest supported story, the main gap to address honestly, two useful questions, and a rehearsal checklist that fits the time remaining. Do not pad it with empty sections.

## Adapt practice to the round

| Round | Preparation emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Recruiter | Relevant background, motivation if known, scope and practical constraints |
| Behavioral or leadership | Personal decisions, tradeoffs, team impact, evidence and lessons |
| Coding or system design | Clarifying requirements, approach, tradeoffs, testing and failure cases |

Label practice questions as plausible exercises based on the role or format, not predictions of an employer's actual questions.

For a mock interview, ask one relevant question and wait. After each answer, give specific feedback on relevance, evidence, clarity, and any round-specific reasoning; suggest one improvement and continue with a follow-up or the next question. If the user wants uninterrupted simulation, save feedback for the end. Do not answer on the candidate's behalf or provide a full packet first.

## Evidence rules

Separate supplied facts, sourced facts, inferences, and unknowns. Preserve personal ownership: participation, coordination, and team outcomes do not establish that the candidate led the work. Help strengthen a truthful account when asked to embellish; do not invent metrics, credentials, motivations, or lessons.

An explicit request to research authorizes relevant public research; do not ask again. Use official company and role sources where possible and cite links alongside the claims they support. If identity is ambiguous, ask for the identifying role or profile link before attributing facts. If browsing is unavailable or the user limits preparation to supplied material, state the limitation and continue with that material. Do not claim knowledge of an interviewer, company priority, interview agenda, or compensation range without evidence.

Return the preparation in the conversation unless the user requests a file or an established workflow specifies a destination. Preparing does not authorize contacting recruiters, submitting applications, or changing calendar events.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Generic advice detached from the role | Tie every recommendation to the role or supplied experience |
| Invented metrics or polished stories | Preserve uncertainty and ask for the missing result later |
| Too many questions | Select the few that change the candidate's decision or preparation |
| Treating practice as a script | Use outlines and proof points in the candidate's own voice |
